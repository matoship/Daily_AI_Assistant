Evaluating Agentic AI: New Frameworks and Playbooks for Production LLMs

Two engineering-focused pieces tackle the operational side of deploying LLM-based agents at scale. An InfoQ presentation outlines how Elastic built reusable evaluation frameworks for agentic AI products, covering how to systematically test and score agent behavior rather than relying on one-off manual checks. A companion InfoQ article lays out a 'platform engineering playbook' for running LLMs in production, addressing infrastructure patterns, observability, and reliability concerns that teams face once generative AI moves beyond prototypes.

Why it matters: As someone building ServiceNow/LLM applications and aiming to become an AI Application Engineer, these are directly applicable: reusable eval frameworks and platform playbooks address exactly the kind of production-readiness and quality-assurance challenges you'll encounter when shipping agentic features, giving concrete patterns you could adapt for your own ServiceNow AI workflows.

New Open-Weight Models Push Coding and Agentic AI Forward

Reflection AI released Beam, a 501B-parameter open-weight mixture-of-experts model with 23B active parameters, targeting coding and agentic workloads. Separately, 'Together Link' launched as a free CLI tool that lets developers run open models like Kimi K3 and GLM 5.3 directly inside popular coding assistants such as Claude Code, Codex, and OpenCode, lowering the barrier to experimenting with alternative open models in existing dev workflows.

Why it matters: These releases expand the open-model toolkit available for building agentic coding assistants—relevant to your work on LLM applications, since large MoE models and easy CLI integrations could offer cost-effective alternatives or benchmarks to compare against the commercial models you may be using in ServiceNow integrations.

Open Model apex-flash-1 Shows Promise in Automated Security Research

Cantina's apex-flash-1, an open model, successfully solved 40 out of 60 held-out bug-finding tasks in a security research benchmark, demonstrating that open models can meaningfully assist in vulnerability discovery and security analysis workflows.

Why it matters: This highlights how open-weight models are increasingly capable of specialized technical reasoning tasks like security research, which is useful context for evaluating model capabilities and trust boundaries when building or securing AI-driven enterprise applications like those on ServiceNow.
