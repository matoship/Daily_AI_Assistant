Agentic AI Is Reshaping Software Engineering Workflows

A cluster of engineering stories shows how agentic AI is moving from novelty to infrastructure: Cloudflare launched persistent, stateful, computer-like environments so AI agents can run long-lived tasks; Spotify presented on using an AI agent to continuously rewrite and migrate its entire codebase; Rootly dropped its 'small PR' rule as AI-assisted code review changes the economics of reviewing larger diffs; Azure API Management introduced a dedicated AI Gateway tier to govern LLM models and MCP tools; and Instacart built 'Blueberry,' an AI assistant that helps on-call engineers investigate production incidents faster.

Why it matters: As someone building toward an AI Application Engineer role and already working with LLM applications on ServiceNow, these are directly relevant signals: Cloudflare's stateful agent environments and Azure's AI Gateway tier show how platforms are standardizing infrastructure for LLM/agent apps you could leverage or compete against; Spotify's and Rootly's stories show real-world shifts in engineering practice (codebase migration agents, PR review economics) driven by AI; Instacart's Blueberry is a concrete example of an AI assistant pattern for ops/incident response that maps closely to enterprise AI application design.

Australia Faces Pressure Over Skilled Migration Suspension

Australia has quietly suspended parts of its offshore skilled worker program, prompting the Migration Council/MCA to demand that planned skilled migration changes be suspended through 2026. A related AFR piece traces the roots of the current migration debate back to a policy decision made in the 1990s that continues to shape today's system.

Why it matters: As a resident of Adelaide, South Australia, changes to skilled migration policy could directly affect visa pathways, offshore hiring, and the broader tech labor market you operate in, including potential effects on skilled worker availability and competition in engineering roles.
