H Company Debuts NeoMME: Lean Single-Tower Multimodal Encoders (260M/800M)

H Company released NeoMME, a family of compact multimodal encoders (260M and 800M parameters) that ditch the traditional separate vision tower and causal decoder in favor of a unified single-tower architecture. This design simplifies the model pipeline while aiming to retain strong multimodal representation quality at a much smaller footprint than typical vision-language models.

Why it matters: As you build toward AI Application Engineering with LLM-based systems, lighter-weight multimodal architectures like this could be directly relevant for embedding efficient vision-language capabilities into applications (e.g., ServiceNow workflows involving document/image understanding) without the overhead of large encoder-decoder stacks.

Meta FAIR's New RPMs Rank ML Experiments Before They Run

Meta FAIR introduced AI Research Preference Models (RPMs), a system designed to predict and rank the likely outcomes of proposed ML experiments before they're actually executed. The goal is to help researchers prioritize promising ideas and avoid wasting expensive GPU compute on low-value experiments.

Why it matters: For engineers working with LLM applications and constrained compute budgets, this kind of experiment-triage approach offers a practical pattern for optimizing R&D cycles — useful inspiration if you're iterating on model fine-tuning or prompt/architecture experiments in your own AI engineering work.

UC Berkeley's CUA-Lite Unifies Tooling for Computer-Use Agents

UC Berkeley researchers released CUA-Lite, an open-source platform that consolidates sandboxing, data collection, evaluation, and reinforcement learning workflows for building and testing computer-use agents (AI agents that operate software/UI environments).

Why it matters: Computer-use agents are increasingly relevant to enterprise automation platforms like ServiceNow. This open framework could serve as a reference architecture or starting point if you explore building agentic automation features that interact with UIs or internal tools.

AI Agents Take On Security: Google's Mantis and Figma's Approach

Two organizations detailed how they're deploying AI agents for security workflows. Google introduced Mantis, an agentic vulnerability scanning harness designed to reduce false positives in automated security scans. Separately, Figma shared how it uses AI agents internally to strengthen its security operations, automating parts of threat detection and response.

Why it matters: As enterprise platforms increasingly embed AI agents into operational workflows, these case studies offer concrete patterns for agentic system design — including reducing noise/false positives and integrating agents into real production security pipelines — directly applicable to building trustworthy LLM-based applications on platforms like ServiceNow.
