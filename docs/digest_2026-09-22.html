Production AI Agents: Locking Down Control with Harnesses, Approval Boundaries, and Identity Frameworks

A trio of engineering pieces converge on the same challenge: how to safely operationalize AI agents in production. An InfoQ presentation details 'agent harness' patterns—control planes, invariants, and approval boundaries that constrain agent behavior. A related podcast dives into the DPACT framework for agent identity and authorization, addressing how agents authenticate and get permissioned to act on systems. Separately, Cloudflare has proposed an 'Agent Development Lifecycle' (ADLC) intended to replace the traditional SDLC for agent-based systems, reflecting how fundamentally different building and shipping agentic software is from conventional application development.

Why it matters: As you build LLM applications on ServiceNow and move toward an AI Application Engineer role, these are exactly the architectural patterns you'll need: how to give agents safe, bounded autonomy, how to handle identity/authorization for agents acting on enterprise systems, and how to rethink your dev lifecycle for agentic apps. This is directly applicable to designing production-grade agent workflows rather than just prototypes.
