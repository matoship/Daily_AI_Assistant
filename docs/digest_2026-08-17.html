New Playbooks for Fine-Tuning and Deploying Small, Tool-Calling LLMs

Several new engineering guides and model releases focus on making LLMs more practical for production agentic use. MarkTechPost published a hands-on guide to streaming, curating, and fine-tuning the SupraLabs Reasoning Corpus to build reasoning-focused LLMs, plus a companion guide on fine-tuning tool-calling models using XYZ-Aquila-SFT and Qwen3. On the model side, Cactus Compute released 'Needle 2', a tiny 45M-parameter tool-calling model that ships as a 14MB binary and runs full sessions in just 28MB of RAM, while Liquid AI released LFM2.5-VL-3B, a 3B vision-language model capable of reading screens, grounding objects, and calling tools on-device.

Why it matters: As an AI Engineer working on ServiceNow/LLM applications with a goal of becoming an AI Application Engineer, these are directly actionable resources: practical fine-tuning workflows for reasoning and tool-calling LLMs, and ultra-lightweight on-device models that could inform architecture decisions for agentic, tool-using applications with tight resource constraints.

AWS Open-Sources Dogwood to Govern Multi-Step Agent Tool Calls

AWS has open-sourced Dogwood, an extension to its Cedar policy language, designed to govern sequences of tool calls made by AI agents rather than just single authorization decisions. This allows developers to define policies that constrain the order, context, and combination of actions an autonomous agent can take.

Why it matters: For LLM application engineering, especially with agentic workflows on platforms like ServiceNow, governance and guardrails around multi-step tool use are increasingly critical for safe deployment. Dogwood offers a concrete pattern for policy-based control over agent behavior that's relevant to building trustworthy AI applications.

Australia's Migration Overhaul Stalls Amid Political and Business Pushback

The Albanese government's planned migration reforms are facing further delays, with Pauline Hanson calling for deeper cuts to intake numbers. Flinders University analysis frames the reforms as being 'at a crossroads.' Meanwhile, business and mining leaders are resisting calls to slash migration intake, and Westpac CEO Anthony Miller has publicly defended migration, arguing Australia 'needs skilled migrants.' A former Treasury boss has also warned that migration cuts could worsen budget pressures.

Why it matters: As a resident of Adelaide, South Australia, shifts in migration policy directly affect skilled visa pathways, including those relevant to tech and engineering roles. The tension between political pressure to cut migration and business/industry demand for skilled migrants is a key trend to watch, particularly for skilled workers in tech-adjacent fields.
