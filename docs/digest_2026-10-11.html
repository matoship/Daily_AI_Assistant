LLMs Learn to Peer-Review Research: Sakana AI's System Flags 73% of Core-Claim Errors

Sakana AI has built an automated peer-review pipeline that uses LLMs to critique scientific papers, successfully catching 73% of core-claim errors in testing. The system is designed to scrutinize the central claims of a paper rather than just surface-level issues, acting as an automated first-pass reviewer.

Why it matters: As an AI engineer building LLM applications, this is a concrete example of using LLMs for structured evaluation and verification tasks rather than just generation. The techniques behind detecting flawed reasoning or unsupported claims could translate directly into building more robust validation layers for your own agentic or decision-support systems on ServiceNow.

New Breed of 'Decision Models' Emerges: Microsoft and Nace AI Release Compact Scorers for Options, Not Text

Two separate releases point to a growing trend of small, specialized 'decision models' built to score and rank options rather than generate free text. Microsoft released Microsoft-Decision-1, a 9B parameter model based on Qwen3.5, while Nace AI open-sourced a similar 9B model called Drex 1.5. Both are positioned as lightweight alternatives to large generative LLMs for decision-scoring tasks.

Why it matters: These compact, task-specific models are directly relevant to building practical AI applications: instead of using a large generative LLM for every decision point in a workflow (e.g., prioritizing incidents or recommending actions in ServiceNow), a smaller dedicated scoring model could offer faster, cheaper, and more reliable decision support — a pattern worth exploring for your own AI application engineering work.

Open-Source Project Lets You Run a Personal AI Agent Free on Cloudflare

Talorys is a new open-source, self-hosted personal AI agent designed to run entirely on Cloudflare's free tier, making it accessible for individual developers to deploy their own always-on AI assistant without hosting costs.

Why it matters: This is a low-cost way to experiment hands-on with agent architecture, hosting, and tool integration patterns — useful side-project material for building toward your goal of becoming an AI Application Engineer, and a good reference for lightweight deployment patterns you could adapt for personal or small-scale ServiceNow-adjacent prototypes.
