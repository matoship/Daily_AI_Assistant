Agent Engineering Roundup: Open-Source Agent Loops, Browser Engines, and Multi-Agent Code Review at Scale

A cluster of engineering stories this week digs into how production AI agent systems are actually built: an open-source course breaks down three architectural patterns for running agent loops and the cost/provider tradeoffs behind each; Cloudflare launched Kitesurf, a purpose-built browser engine for AI agents; LinkedIn detailed a multi-agent system it uses for automated code review at scale; AWS released 'Aws-Bench,' a benchmark suite for evaluating how well agents perform real cloud engineering tasks; and DoorDash presented SafeChat, its architecture for real-time LLM-based safety/moderation systems in a live marketplace.

Why it matters: These are directly relevant to your work building LLM applications on ServiceNow and your goal of becoming an AI Application Engineer. The agent-loop economics piece and AWS-Bench give you concrete frameworks for evaluating agent architectures and costs, LinkedIn's multi-agent code review approach offers a real-world pattern you could adapt for engineering workflows, Cloudflare's Kitesurf shows emerging infra for agents that browse/act on the web, and DoorDash's SafeChat is a solid case study in scaling reliable, real-time LLM systems in production — all patterns transferable to enterprise LLM app design.

Australia's Migration Policy Under Strain: Delays, Slower Visa Processing, and Political Blame Game

Three related stories highlight growing friction in Australia's migration system: the Australian Financial Review reports that economic considerations, not just policy design, drove Labor's decision to delay its migration policy overhaul; separately, work visa processing times in Australia have stretched out to roughly one year, causing frustration among applicants and employers; and commentary from Australian Rural & Regional News criticizes Canberra for expanding migration intake while publicly blaming backpackers and temporary visa holders for downstream pressures (e.g., housing, regional labor shortages) that the policy itself contributed to.

Why it matters: As someone based in Adelaide navigating Australia's visa and immigration environment, these stories are directly relevant — longer work visa processing times and shifting migration policy can affect your own visa status, employer sponsorship timelines, and broader job market conditions in tech/engineering roles that rely on skilled migration pathways.
